# Physical scope of the registered ansatzes

The controls use published STO-3G hydrogen (four spin orbitals, two electrons) and the saved four-qubit contextual projection of glycine. A normalized state can reproduce its energy on the given qubit Hamiltonian while breaking a claimed charge, spin or circuit constraint. On a contextual projection, the physical electron and spin operators also require transformation before their expectations can be interpreted. The bit count of the reduced register has no general electron-count interpretation. [Contextual state mapping](../contextual_subspace/cs_reduction.py#L94) · [Manuscript discussion, §2.4](../../QMProt.pdf)

| Registered method | Measured behavior and affected interpretation |
|---|---|
| PennyLane VQE | Two layers of RX/RY/RZ and fixed CNOTs give zero HF fidelity on hydrogen at zero rotation angles, with energy −0.4168273600 Ha (HF is −1.1175058842). Its 40-evaluation endpoint had 40.4% outside the two-electron sector. This affects near-HF initialization and sector-specific energy comparisons. [Circuit](../algorithms/vqe_pennylane.py#L68) |
| Hardware-efficient VQE | The two-layer CNOT ring at zero rotation maps the HF determinant to a different bit string with energy +0.4194096897 Ha. The seeded 40-evaluation endpoint had 17.0% outside the sector. Targeting angles near zero does not ensure a near-HF start. This directly affects the HE trainability, initialization and noise experiments. [Circuit](../algorithms/vqe_hardware_efficient.py#L119) |
| Fermionic ADAPT | The registered single/double gate sequences use RY and CNOTs. The first double on hydrogen, labelled (0,1,1,2), maps the two-electron HF state entirely into the one-electron sector at angle zero. The previous circuit is therefore absent at the zero-angle point used for its gradient screen. The method is unsuitable as a fermionic ADAPT baseline under that label. [Pool and appended gates](../algorithms/vqe_adapt.py#L73) · [Central difference](../algorithms/vqe_adapt.py#L140) |
| Qubit-ADAPT | The restricted hydrogen pool has 12 Pauli generators. Applying two allowed rotations `Y0 X2` and `Y0 X3`, each at parameter π/4, puts 25% outside N=2. Qubit-ADAPT reached the correct sector at the measured hydrogen endpoint, but its pool does not enforce it. In four-qubit contextual space its larger pool contains 20 odd-Y two- and four-wire words; “full pool” in these experiments refers to those combinations. This distinction applies to the November trainability campaign. [Pool definitions](../algorithms/vqe_qubit_adapt.py#L102) · [CS dispatch](../algorithms/vqe_qubit_adapt.py#L193) · [Rotation convention](../algorithms/vqe_qubit_adapt.py#L246) |
| QAOA-inspired VQE | Fixed angles gave identical statevectors for hydrogen and the glycine projection. The cost layers implement a fixed chain with generator ΣZᵢ + ½ΣZᵢZᵢ₊₁. The molecular Hamiltonian enters at readout. The “XY” mixer is a product of single-qubit RX and RY gates. At β=0.6 its action alone moved 42.8% of HF outside the two-electron sector. Compare this circuit as a hardware-native ansatz; a molecular cost-evolution or number-conserving XY claim would require different gates. [Cost and mixers](../algorithms/vqe_qaoa_inspired.py#L78) |
| iQCC-inspired | The public cost and internal fit now score the supplied angles. On hydrogen at 0.73 with Y₀, both give −0.8550212993 Ha. The internal fit uses fixed COBYLA and up to 100 evaluations per operator. Its metadata reports that policy, molecular objective evaluations, screening-observable expectations, commutator constructions and accepted generator names. The weight-one/two pool has zero initial gradients on unrotated hydrogen even though a weight-four Pauli gradient has magnitude 0.179669 Ha/radian in the half-angle convention. A threshold stop leaves 19.341 mHa of correlation energy here. [Public cost and screening](../algorithms/vqe_iqcc_inspired.py#L124) · [Inner optimizer](../algorithms/vqe_iqcc_inspired.py#L203) |
| True iQCC | The corrected Hamiltonian transform and original-H circuit satisfy the independent dense-unitary checks. The historical glycine prefix at 26 terms undershot its spectrum by 11.093333 mHa; the corrected result was 1.7×10⁻¹³ Ha above the independent minimum. Its weight-one/two pool has the same hydrogen first-screen limitation. Unpruned Pauli dressing can grow as far as 4ⁿ terms on n qubits. [Dressing and score](../algorithms/vqe_iqcc_true.py) · [Historical sources and fixture](reproductions/iqcc_glycine/README.md) |
| Classically boosted VQE | The claimed target is a complex two-state Rayleigh–Ritz solve using the encoded HF reference and the optimized state. Before correction, the measured glycine optimum differed by 0.173186 mHa between real and complex coefficients **even in the same two-dimensional span**. Replacing its classical bit string with the encoded HF changed the span as well (fidelity zero). The current solver uses complex overlaps and prepares the actual reference. Check the boosted energy against that span and check the underlying trial energy separately. [Current boost](../algorithms/vqe_CB.py#L188) · [Independent check](physics_acceptance.py) |
| HVA | On hydrogen the two commuting groups summed to the original Hamiltonian exactly. Gate-state fidelity to independently exponentiated dense group matrices was 0.999999999999992. The first six glycine terms produce one commuting group. Both nominal layer angles then leave the objective invariant for every state, since both unitaries commute with H. This matters when interpreting early prefix-sweep points and parameter counts. [Grouping](../algorithms/vqe_hva.py#L82) · [Evolution](../algorithms/vqe_hva.py#L241) |
| UCC | The default paired singles use bare, nonadjacent qubit Givens rotations. On two-electron hydrogen at angles (π/2, 0), the qubit circuit preserves N but produces ⟨S²⟩=1.0 (50% triplet). The corresponding spin-summed fermionic generator, including Jordan–Wigner signs, gives ⟨S²⟩=0.0. The measured hydrogen endpoint reached the correct energy; that single endpoint cannot establish spin symmetry. The “UCCSD” label also needs its paired-doubles restriction and ordered product specified. Occupied/virtual labels on contextual qubits are heuristic. [Excitations](../algorithms/vqe_ucc.py#L59) · [Gates and spin pairing](../algorithms/vqe_ucc.py#L282) |
| NN-AE | The full register remains live in the encoder, latent block and decoder. With seeded hydrogen angles, the two-qubit reduced state after encoding had purity 0.69537. Discarding the other two qubits would change the state. The measured mirrored default at zero angles reproduced HF. The configurable independent decoder at zero angles had HF fidelity zero. Report this as a unitary encoder/decoder circuit with a latent gate block; a qubit-compression or reconstruction claim requires a separate criterion. [Architecture controls](../algorithms/vqe_nn_ae.py#L239) · [Circuit](../algorithms/vqe_nn_ae.py#L390) |

The 40-evaluation endpoints above used original solver settings for two nominal layers, three allowed adaptive operators, seed 42 and a gradient threshold of 10⁻⁸. Specific nonstationary controls were chosen to test the stated algebra; they do not estimate how often an unconstrained optimization visits those states. For the six supported noisy fixed circuits, independent density-matrix propagation at channel strength 0.02 confirmed 42 baseline, zero-strength and noisy CSV rows within 5×10⁻¹¹ for the energy and L2 drift columns. That check tests the simulated channels at their configured insertion points. [Independent channel check](test_noise_physics.py)

Run the following from `framework/` with the installed scientific environment. The first command prints the controlled measurements. The second saves per-solver scores, warnings and violations for both molecules, and exits nonzero when a physical assertion fails.

## Matched fermionic ADAPT control

The opt-in control shares the registered method's two-sided finite-difference screen, COBYLA, 10⁻⁸ gradient threshold, seed 42, three allowed additions, 100 optimizer evaluations per inner solve and a 300-energy cap. Both molecules use the original spin-orbital basis with two active electrons. Glycine uses only the canonical HOMO and LUMO (zero-based indices 19 and 20), with the other occupied orbitals frozen. That control targets its own CASCI energy, −279.1115600159873 Ha. [Control and matched Fock-space exponentials](fermionic_adapt.py) · [Input hashes and recorded results](reproductions/fermionic_adapt/observations.json)

| Molecule | Registered approximate gates, 12 candidates | Same approximate gates, 3 matched candidates | JW fermionic gates, same 3 candidates |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| H₂ | 19.340691 mHa error, 26 energy evaluations | 19.340691 mHa, 8 evaluations | <10⁻⁸ mHa, 55 evaluations, 1 addition |
| Glycine CAS(2,2) | 0.04528765 mHa, 26 evaluations | 0.04528765 mHa, 8 evaluations | <10⁻⁸ mHa, 190 evaluations, 3 additions |

The approximate runs stopped on the first gradient screen and returned HF, with zero charge leakage at that endpoint. Its native pool includes six “double” tuples, four with a repeated index; their gate wires describe two CNOTs on shifted pairs. The three matched labels are the two spin-preserving occupied-to-virtual singles and the one pair double. All were screened in both matched-pool runs. The zero-gradient scans consumed one baseline evaluation, two trial evaluations per candidate and one final HF evaluation. The JW runs used additional calls for accepted-angle optimization and subsequent screens.

On the same hydrogen determinant, with exactly the same occupied modes (0,1), virtual modes (2,3) and angle 0.4 radians, the approximate double trial had 0.009478 fidelity against the dense JW state and 98.03% wrong-charge probability. At zero angle it was orthogonal to HF. The genuine JW double had unit fidelity to numerical precision at both angles. Independent unitary tests also compare every matrix element for both singles and the double at 0, +0.4 and −0.83 radians. These trial-state defects and the charge of the final stopped state are separate measurements. [Dense-unitary tests](test_fermionic_adapt.py)
